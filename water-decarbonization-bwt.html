  • FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

    * **Q: Why is Kyiv municipal tap water particularly dangerous for professional espresso machines?**

    *A:* Kyiv’s municipal water is exceptionally hard, registering between 15 and 20 dH. This water is saturated with calcium and magnesium ions. When heated inside a professional boiler to extract espresso, these minerals instantly precipitate out of the liquid, creating a hard crystalline rock known as limescale. This scale coats heating elements, clogs high-pressure solenoid valves, and insulates thermal sensors, leading to rapid boiler failure and voided manufacturer warranties within 6 months of use.

    * **Q: Can we just use a standard countertop water filter pitcher or a simple inline carbon filter?**

    *A:* Absolutely not. Standard carbon and countertop filters are designed to remove organic odors, chlorine, and sediment to improve basic taste. They have zero chemical capacity to reduce water hardness. To soften water and prevent scale, you must deploy a dedicated ion-exchange filtration system that physically swaps calcium and magnesium ions for sodium ions, calibrated specifically to handle Kyiv’s extreme mineral levels.

    * **Q: How does water hardness affect the actual taste of high-quality specialty coffee?**

    *A:* Water makes up 95% to 98% of your espresso. When your water is saturated with raw minerals (15–20 dH), it is chemically "full" and cannot dissolve and extract the desirable oils, natural sugars, and pleasant organic acids from your coffee beans. This results in under-extracted, flat, sour, or metallic-tasting coffee. By softening the water to 4.5 dH, we allow the water to act as a clean solvent, unlocking the rich, balanced, and premium flavor profile of our specialty coffee.
