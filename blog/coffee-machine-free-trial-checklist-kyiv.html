Free coffee-machine trial: how to test fairly on your own volume

A free 14-day trial only helps if you measure the right things. A practical checklist for Kyiv offices, shops and HoReCa venues: what to record before day one, what to watch in week one and how to decide.

A trial is the lowest-risk way to choose a coffee-machine supplier, but only if you know what you are testing. A machine that sits in the corner for two weeks and gets "a general impression" tells you very little. A trial with a written baseline and a short scorecard tells you almost everything.

Below is the routine we suggest for any supplier, including Anima Volitiva, where the trial is free for 14 days and needs no prepayment.

1. Write down your baseline before day one

Note how your current setup performs: roughly how many drinks go out on a normal day, when the queue is longest, how many complaints you hear in a week, and how long you waited the last time something broke. Rough numbers from your own team are enough. Without a baseline you will end up comparing a feeling with a feeling.

2. Test in real conditions, not in the showroom

Put the machine where it will actually live and run it through your busiest hour. Delivery and installation with us take place within 24 hours, so you can plan the start for a Monday morning rather than a quiet afternoon. Involve the people who complain most about coffee today: they are your best testers.

3. Check whether your team can run it without help

Staff training is part of our onboarding, which has five stages: planning, setup, training, a first-week quality check and a review after the first month. During the trial, see whether your own people can handle the daily routine (filling, cleaning, simple errors) without calling anyone. If they cannot after training, the machine or the training is the problem, not the staff.

4. Test the service, not only the coffee

Coffee quality is only half of the decision. Call the support line at an awkward hour and ask what happens if the machine stops. Our commitment is a 24/7 support line and a technician on site or a replacement machine within 24 hours. Whatever supplier you test, get the breakdown promise in writing and ask who exactly turns up.

5. Look at data, not only at impressions

Ask the supplier what you will be able to see. Our service includes an online analytics dashboard with cups sold, revenue, stock and errors. Compare it with your baseline from step 1: did the queue shorten, did complaints drop, did anyone run out of something at the wrong moment.

6. Decide with a scorecard, then ask for the quote

Score each point from 1 to 5: coffee quality, ease of use for staff, speed in the peak hour, service response, clarity of the offer. Only then talk about money. We quote individually for your venue and volume, with one of three equipment packages (Start, Pro or Max) matched to the format, so the quote should be the last step, not the first.
